# Robert M. Pirsig: a short biography

Robert Maynard Pirsig (6 September 1928 to 24 April 2017) was an American writer and
philosopher. He is thinker 30 of [`50THINKERS.md`](50THINKERS.md), and the author this book will
cite most ([ADR 001](docs/2-engineering/adr/001-pirsig-is-the-most-cited-author.md)).

**One source.** Every fact below was read from one page: Wikipedia, "Robert M. Pirsig", revision
[1377828662](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Robert_M._Pirsig&oldid=1377828662) of 1
October 2026, read on 14 October 2026. An encyclopedia is a start and not a proof: before a fact
of this page goes into the book, check it against the source that the encyclopedia names.

## The life, in order

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 1928 | Born in Minneapolis, Minnesota. His father taught law at the University of Minnesota and was dean of its law school |
| 1943 | A high school diploma at 14 |
| 1940s | Studied biochemistry at the University of Minnesota. He asked where hypotheses come from, his grades fell, and the university expelled him |
| 1946 to 1948 | In the United States Army, in South Korea |
| 1950 | Back at the University of Minnesota: a bachelor's degree |
| 1950s | Studied philosophy at Banaras Hindu University, in India, and at the University of Chicago |
| 1958 | A master's degree in journalism. He taught writing at Montana State College, in Bozeman, for two years |
| 1961 to 1963 | A mental breakdown; time in psychiatric hospitals. He wrote about the treatment in his first book |
| 1960s | A technical writer |
| July 1968 | The motorcycle journey with his son Chris, from Minnesota to California |
| 1968 to 1974 | Nearly four years of writing. He offered the book to 121 publishers |
| 1974 | *Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance: An Inquiry into Values* (William Morrow). A Guggenheim Fellowship |
| 1979 | His son Chris was killed in San Francisco, at 22 |
| 1991 | *Lila: An Inquiry into Morals*. It sold less than the first book; he held it to be the more important |
| 2012 | An honorary doctorate from Montana State University |
| 2017 | Died at 88, at his home in South Berwick, Maine |
| 2022 | *On Quality*, selected and unpublished writings, with Wendy Pirsig, his wife and editor |

## Three things to keep

1. **He was a technical writer who wrote a book of philosophy.** The book started in 1968 as an
   idea for an essay on how he fixed motorcycles.
2. **121 publishers.** The count is the encyclopedia's. The book was published, and became the
   book that this repository's [`BOOKS.md`](BOOKS.md) puts first.
3. **Quality.** Both books ask what Quality is. `BOOKS.md` says how this repository reads the
   word; the essay [New vs. better, 2026](essays/2026-new-vs-better.md) uses his two questions,
   "What's new?" and "What is best?".

## What this page does not say

It gives no judgement of his philosophy, and nothing about his family beyond what the
encyclopedia states plainly. It does not say that he is "the legend": that word is the owner's
