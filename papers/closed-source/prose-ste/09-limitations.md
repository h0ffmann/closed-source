# Limitations {#sec:limits}

I state the limits of my argument clearly. This agrees with the value of honesty about
results.

- **Position, not measurement.** This paper uses cited evidence for its argument. It does not
  report a new experiment. Its claims about the cost of reverse engineering come from the cited
  studies, not from my own measurements.
- **Allegations, not findings.** The Apple v. OpenAI dispute and the distillation accusations, which
  include the advisory of three US agencies, are allegations. When I wrote this paper, no court had
  made a decision about their merits. The Ding case is different: it ended in a conviction.
- **Secondary sources for 2026 events.** For most 2026 events, my sources are news reports. I read
  the Apple complaint in a copy that a news service published, not from PACER. I read the joint
  advisory AA26-251A through two reports, not from CISA. Reports of later hearings can use words
  that the court would not use.
- **A record that changes.** The trial of the Apple case is in July 2027, and the request to
  dismiss was not decided when I wrote this paper. Each of the 2026 events can look different when
  a reader sees this paper.
- **Star counts are snapshots.** I give the popularity of REA and the Craft projects as I read it
  on one day. Such counts change quickly, and people can inflate them. I use them as a sign of
  attention, not of quality.
- **Not legal advice.** Section [-@sec:law] quotes three laws and gives a plain reading. It does
  not analyse case law. It does not say if a given rebuild is lawful.
- **Not all closedness is wrong.** Patient data, personal data, security-sensitive details and
  material under embargo have good reasons to stay closed. My argument is about methods, code and
  models whose secrecy protects only their owner.
- **Author's interest.** I build open-source tools and cite my own projects. Readers
  should consider this interest when they evaluate Section [-@sec:related].
- **The anecdote of the author is missing.** One passage in Section [-@sec:related] has a marker
  for the words of the author, and the author did not write it yet.
- **A machine checks the translations, not a second translator.** The check of the ASD-STE100
  edition and of the Brazilian Portuguese edition compares each file with the English file. It
  compares the citations, anchors, quotations, numbers and markers. It finds a lost citation, a
  changed date or an added number. It cannot find a sentence that keeps each number but says a
  different thing. A human translator did not review the two editions yet.
- **Not yet peer reviewed.** This is a v0 preprint.
