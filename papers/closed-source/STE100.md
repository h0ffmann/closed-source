# ASD-STE100 passes

`prose-ste/` is the paper of `prose/` in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, issue 9, made
with the rules of [`/ste100`](../../.claude/commands/ste100.md) on 2026-10-12, after the method of
h0ffmann/picasso's `docs/whitepapers/determinism-2026/STE100.md`. `just` builds it to
`pdf/closed-source-ste.pdf`; `prose/` stays as written and builds to `pdf/closed-source.pdf`.

These items did not change, and `scripts/ste_check.py` (run by `just check`) fails if one does:
each citation marker (same keys, same counts), each anchor, each quotation, each number, and the
`[ANALYSIS]` and `[AUTHOR ...]` markers. The bibliography is shared, so the references are
identical.

## Pass 1: rewrite

Two agents rewrote the paper in two halves (abstract and sections 1 to 5; sections 7 to 11).

- They split compound sentences and paragraphs of more than six sentences; one analysis paragraph
  in section 3 became two, each with its `[ANALYSIS]` marker.
- They removed -ing forms and phrasal verbs, used simple tenses, and used the active voice where
  the agent is known.
- They wrote the three agent rules of section 7 in the imperative, with the condition first.
- They used one term for one meaning: "closed source" (also for "proprietary"), "reverse
  engineering", "secrecy", "agent", "model", "persistent identifier".

## Pass 2: check

One agent read the full text against the rules and the original, wrote the STE text of section 6
(added to the original during pass 1), and brought in the other additions made during pass 1 (one
abstract paragraph, the nashkell item in section 9). It made 16 edits.

- Term unification (4): "lawsuit", "checked", "fetch", "When we wrote this paper".
- Meanings that pass 1 changed, restored from the original (8): "many eyes find bugs that few
  would" (pass 1 had reversed it); "an accusation of industrial copying"; "writing styles" (a
  name); the four developments *show* that the cost curve collapsed, not *cause* it; five author
  placeholders restored word for word.
- Length and paragraphs (3), word forms (1).

One edit after pass 2: in rule 1 of section 7, "is not a borrow" became "is not borrowing".

## Kept outside the rules

- Sentences over 25 words: verbatim quotations (Torvalds, OpenAI's memo, Kerckhoffs, Ince, Nash),
  and the author placeholders, which only the author may reword.
- -ing words in fixed phrases and terms: "According to", "pending", "working paper", "embedding",
  "machine learning", "software engineering", "signing key", "Competing interests", and in the
  placeholders.
- "should" where the original says "should" (the FORCE11 principles in section 7, and section 10); "must" would make the
  claim stronger.
