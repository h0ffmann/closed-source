# Limitations {#sec:limits}

I state the limits of my argument plainly, in line with the value of honesty about
results.

- **Position, not measurement.** This paper argues from cited evidence; it reports no new
  experiment. Its claims about the cost of reverse engineering rest on the studies cited, not on
  my own measurements.
- **Allegations, not findings.** The Apple v. OpenAI dispute and the distillation accusations,
  including the advisory of three US agencies, are allegations. No court had ruled on their merits
  at the time of writing. The Ding case is the exception: it ended in a conviction.
- **Secondary sources for 2026 events.** I relied on news reports for most 2026 events. The Apple
  complaint was read in a copy published by a news service, not from PACER. The joint advisory
  AA26-251A was read through two reports, not from CISA. Reports of later hearings may describe
  them in words the court would not use.
- **A moving record.** The Apple case is set for trial in July 2027, and the motion to dismiss was
  pending when I wrote. Any of the 2026 events may look different by the time a reader sees this.
- **Star counts are snapshots.** The popularity of REA and the Craft projects is given as read on
  one day. Such counts move fast and can be inflated; I use them as a sign of attention, not of
  quality.
- **Not legal advice.** Section [-@sec:law] quotes three statutes and draws a plain reading. It does
  not analyse case law, and it does not say whether any particular rebuild is lawful.
- **Not all closedness is wrong.** Patient data, personal data, security-sensitive details and
  material under embargo have good reasons to stay closed. My argument concerns methods, code
  and models whose secrecy protects only their owner.
- **Author's interest.** I build open-source tools and cite my own projects. Readers
  should weigh Section [-@sec:related] accordingly.
- **A single author's anecdote is missing.** One passage in Section [-@sec:related] is marked for
  the author's own words and is not yet written.
- **The translations are checked by a machine, not by a second translator.** The check of the
  ASD-STE100 and Brazilian Portuguese editions compares, file by file, the citations, anchors,
  quotations, numbers and markers of each edition with the English one. It catches a lost
  citation, a changed date or an added figure. It cannot catch a sentence that keeps every number
  and still says something different. A human translator has not yet reviewed either edition.
- **Not yet peer reviewed.** This is a v0 preprint.
