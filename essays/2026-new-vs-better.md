# New vs. better, 2026

*A draft. Claude derived it from the owner's notes ([`DERIVATION.md`](../DERIVATION.md)). Each
"I" below stands on a note that the owner gave; where a note is missing, a marker says what the
owner has to write. It is written in October 2026 and it says how things look this month.*

In 2017 I learned a language because a tool failed. Three of us had a risk model in R, Spark
would not run it, and so I wrote a part of it again in Scala. I did not choose Scala because it
was new. I chose it because it worked. In the same months I was taking Latin classes, and nobody
understood why, not even I. A man laughed when I told him. It took me years to see that he was
laughing with me.

I begin there because of a book that I go back to. Robert Pirsig opens his talks with two
questions. "What's new?", he writes, "is an interesting and broadening eternal question, but one
which, if pursued exclusively, results only in an endless parade of trivia and fashion, the silt
of tomorrow." He would like to ask another one: "What is best?", "a question which cuts deeply
rather than broadly". He wrote that in 1974. I say *better* where he says *best*, because I can
compare two things and I cannot rank them all. I try his two questions on five things of 2026.

**AI.** This month everybody talks about one subject, reverse engineering with agents, and I ran
to write about it. That was the first question, and I followed it. In the same days an agent and
I moved the tools of this book from Python to Gleam: 8,222 lines added, 1,647 removed. Gleam is
the newer language of the two. Is it the better one? What I can show is small. Each new tool
gave the same output as the script it replaced. The same output is "not worse". Better is what I
will know in a year, when I have had to maintain it.

**El Niño.** Nothing in this list is older. On 8 October 2026 the Climate Prediction Center of
NOAA wrote that "El Niño continues to strengthen", and that a strong one, or a stronger one, is
likely to last through January to March 2027. It comes back, and each time it is told as news. For a forecast
that people act on, I wrote a rule down: the forecast in operations is the one that wins the
benchmark, and a new model is tried in simulation, where a bad pick costs nothing. That rule is
Pirsig's second question made into a procedure. I still feel the pull of the new model.

**Social media.** A feed asks only the first question. It is the "endless parade" in a literal
form. My own rule goes the other way: I only need to be seen by the right people, so I choose
the words that the right reader would type. I do not know if that rule is better or only
quieter. **[AUTHOR ANECDOTE: what social media gave you or cost you in 2026, in one case.]**

**Bitcoin.** Its paper proposed payments "sent directly from one party to another without going
through a financial institution". That was new. I have not done the work to say if it is
better, or for whom. **[AUTHOR ANECDOTE: your position on Bitcoin in 2026, and the one fact that
moved it.]**

**Privacy.** Here my own book seems to stand against me. I argue that closed source failed. Yet
in my notes my friends have no names: they are friend1, friend4, prof1. One man has his name
there, because I asked for it. I think that this old thing is the better one, and not only the
older one: open work, private people. A program can be opened and nothing is lost. A person who
was named cannot be un-named. Where the line goes between the two, I cannot say exactly.

What do I know? Five things, and of none can I say "this is best". I notice one thing only. In
each of them the new announced itself, and the better had to be checked later, by somebody who
stayed to maintain it. I will read this page again in October 2027, and I may contradict it.
