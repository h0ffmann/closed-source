# Peer reviewing: the god that failed

*A draft, derived by Claude from the owner's notes ([`DERIVATION.md`](../DERIVATION.md)), for one
printed page. Written in October 2026.*

I published a book this month and nobody reviewed it. I thought of a group that could, and I
know of nobody there who reviews this. So I did what the rite forbids: I printed first.

I wanted to write that peer review began in the wrong way, with the wrong person. The record
does not let me say it so. The first referee on record is of 1665. What is young is the rule
that everybody must pass him: it "did not become commonplace until the mid-20th-century", and
*Nature* took it up in 1967. And what a person began is the business. Robert Maxwell "turned
scientific journals into a spectacular money-making machine", and named it himself: "a
perpetual financing machine". The machine runs on a gift. The checking "is done by working
scientists on a volunteer basis". When Maxwell died, "an investigation revealed that he had
stolen more than £400m" from a pension fund. I cannot say that the theft and the journals are
one thing. I say that the same hand built both.

In Kubrick's last film a doctor walks into a house where everybody wears a mask. It is a
secret society, and he does not belong to it. As I see that house, nobody in it asks if a thing
is true. They ask if you belong. I think of it when I read that a paper waits for referees
whose names its author may not know.

What do I know? I cannot say what a field needs when its readers are many and their time is
short. Some rite may be needed there. But a check that I cannot open is closed source, and I wrote a book
against that. Peer reviewing is a ritual.
