# What is distillation

The owner's word, as it came: **distillation is the massification of computation.**

**What distillation is (the fact).** In machine learning, distillation trains a small model to do
what a large model, or a group of models, does. Hinton, Vinyals and Dean named the method in
"Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network" (arXiv:1503.02531, 9 March 2015, read 15 October
2026): it can "compress the knowledge in an ensemble into a single model". They credit the first
form of the idea to Caruana and his co-authors.

**[ANALYSIS] The owner's reading.** What one large and costly model can do, distillation makes
small and cheap, so that it can run everywhere and for everyone. The few, expensive computations
become many, common ones: computation for the mass. A distilled model copies the behaviour of
its teacher, not its source; see [[202610141844-art-is-dead-the-age-of-copy]] and
[[202610132128-not-against-property-copy-professionally]].

**Toward Ortega y Gasset.** The word "massification" points to José Ortega y Gasset's theory of
the masses, *La rebelión de las masas* (*The Revolt of the Masses*; in *El Sol* in 1929, a book
in 1930, as Wikipedia's article on the book says, read 15 October 2026). Notes on Ortega come
later. The open question for them: is a distilled model Ortega's "mass-man" of computation, a
common copy that rises to power without the effort of the original?
