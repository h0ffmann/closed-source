# A TV audience is a competition, and I think in game theory

The idea, as it came. As a contractor for a television broadcaster, I was estimating its
audience. The obvious way is history: what the broadcaster's own programmes drew on channel Y,
Z, K, and so on. A better way to measure a channel's power is to look at its competition. A TV
audience is a competition: the viewers one channel holds are viewers the others do not, at the
same hour. So the estimate should model the rivals and their schedules, not only the channel's
own past.

I was only a contractor, so it was not mine to push. The idea still sounds good to me.

The game theory came later, only at the end. Looking back, the idea already had its shape: a
fixed audience shared among players who choose what to air, each choice changing what the others
get. That is a game. Now I am fairly sure that I really think in game-theory terms.

**What this note does not claim.** No numbers, no broadcaster's name and no model are given; it
records how I reasoned, not a result. Whether a competition model beats a channel's own history
as an estimate is open.
