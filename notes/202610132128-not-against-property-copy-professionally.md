# Not against property: if you copy, copy professionally

The book does not say that everything must be closed. It is not against private property. Its
point is narrower: if you copy, copy in a professional way.

I think a Japanese or a Chinese reader would understand this more easily, because they think
about the long term. My example to friend3 was that the Chinese models are all open.

I am also not the first person to speak about open source and science together.

**Status: open question.** The first paragraph is my position. The rest is checked below, claim
by claim.

**What I am speaking of.**

- The book: *Closed Source: The God that Failed* (Hoffmann, 2026), DOI
[10.5281/zenodo.23333656](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23333656); to read,
  <https://h0ffmann.github.io/closed-source/closed-source.pdf> (the link I sent; it answered
  with a PDF on 13 October 2026).
- "Copy professionally" is the rule this repository keeps as "borrow, cite, propagate"
  ([`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md)): read the licence, cite at the point of use, pass the credit on.
- "Not the first": verified. The book's Zenodo record cites three works by other people that
  made the case before me ([`RELATED_WORK.md`](../RELATED_WORK.md); each is `verified` in
  `papers/closed-source/REFERENCES.md`):
  - Ince, Hatton and Graham-Cumming (2012), "The case for open computer programs", *Nature*,
    [10.1038/nature10836](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10836).
  - Smith, Katz, Niemeyer and others (2016), "Software citation principles", *PeerJ Computer
    Science*, [10.7717/peerj-cs.86](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj-cs.86).
  - Barker, Chue Hong, Katz and others (2022), "Introducing the FAIR Principles for research
    software", *Scientific Data*,
    [10.1038/s41597-022-01710-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01710-x).
- "The Chinese models are all open": partial, and "all" is not shown. Caiwei Chen, "What's next
  for Chinese open-source AI", *MIT Technology Review*, 12 February 2026
  (<https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/02/12/1132811/whats-next-for-chinese-open-source-ai/>,
  read on 13 October 2026), reports that Chinese companies publish their models' weights, and
  names DeepSeek, Alibaba's Qwen, Z.ai, MiniMax and Tencent among them. I give no quotation,
  because the page was read through a summary. The article does not say that every Chinese
  model is open. A count of the main Chinese models and their licences, on a stated date, would
  settle it.
- That readers in Japan or China take the long view is my impression. No source was looked for.
