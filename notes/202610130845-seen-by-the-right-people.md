# You only need to be seen by the right people

Open work does not need a crowd. It needs to reach the few people who can use it, check it or
build on it. For this paper those people are reverse engineers. So the words that describe the
work are chosen for them, not for the largest number of searches.

This is the policy behind [`KEYWORDS.md`](../KEYWORDS.md): a keyword is kept when the right
reader would type it, and when the paper is about it. A keyword that only brings more visitors is
left out.

It is the same spirit as [[202610130121-what-makes-the-work-japanese]]: the work is made in the
open and gives credit, and it does not shout. It is the opposite of the reach that
[[202610122238-cloud-capture-of-open-source]] describes, where the largest party takes the
attention.

**How it is seen.** The work is on Zenodo, and Zenodo is read. A record there is not alone: it
is tied to other records, and the ties are public.

- Each keyword on the record is a link to every other record with the same keyword, so a reader
  of someone else's work on reverse engineering is one click from this one.
- The record names its related works, and DataCite keeps those relations. For v0.2.0 it holds
  six: three to works of mine and three to works of other people (Ince, Hatton and
  Graham-Cumming 2012; Smith, Katz and Niemeyer 2016; Barker and others 2022).

[`RELATED_WORK.md`](../RELATED_WORK.md) lists every relation with the date it was recorded.

**Status: verified** for the two mechanisms above: seen on
<https://zenodo.org/records/23335204> and in DataCite's record of
[10.5281/zenodo.23335204](https://api.datacite.org/dois/10.5281/zenodo.23335204), which was
registered on 13 October 2026 at 07:38 UTC. The first paragraph is my own position, not a fact
to verify.
