# The Ouroboros: the myth of the serpent that eats its tail

- **Name.** Greek *ouroboros*: *oura*, tail, and *-boros*, eating (Liddell & Scott, 1940).
- **Egypt.** The first known picture is on a shrine around Tutankhamun's sarcophagus, in the
  *Enigmatic Book of the Netherworld*, 14th century BCE; it probably shows Ra and Osiris as one
  (Hornung, *The Ancient Egyptian Books of the Afterlife*, 1999).
- **Gnosticism and alchemy.** A serpent biting its tail stood for eternity and the soul of the
  world (Origen, *Contra Celsum* 6.25). The *Chrysopoeia of Cleopatra* (probably 3rd-century
  Alexandria, known from a 10th-century copy) writes "hen to pan", "the all is one", inside it.
- **Norse.** Jörmungandr, a child of Loki, grows until it circles the world and bites its tail
  (the article gives no citation here).
- **Jung** called it an archetype and "a dramatic symbol for the integration and assimilation of
  the opposite, i.e. of the shadow" (*Collected Works* 14, para. 513).
