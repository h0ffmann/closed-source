# Pirsig: the hypotheses are infinite, so science does not close

What one book says about science, read again on 18 October 2026.

**Where.** In chapter 10 of *Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance* (1974), Phaedrus, the
man Pirsig was before his breakdown, studies biochemistry and finds a fault in the method he
was taught. The biography in [`BIO_ROBERT_PIRSIG.md`](../BIO_ROBERT_PIRSIG.md) gives the same
event from the life: "He asked where hypotheses come from, his grades fell".

**What he says, in four sentences.**

1. What the method is for: "The purpose of scientific method is to select a single truth from
   among many hypothetical truths."
2. What he saw instead: "The number of rational hypotheses that can explain any given
   phenomenon is infinite." (p. 107 of the 1974 edition.)
3. Why it does not stop: "For every fact there is an infinity of hypotheses. The more you look
   the more you see." (p. 171.)
4. What the method then does: "Instead of selecting one truth from a multitude you are
   increasing the multitude."

**In my words.** The method was made to close a question. Each experiment opens more questions
than it closes. So science is not a building that one day is finished. It is a field that grows
as you walk on it. Pirsig did not give up the method: on pp. 100 to 101 he says that its "real
purpose" is "to make sure Nature hasn't misled you into thinking you know something you don't".

**His views, refreshed for 2026.** Pirsig died in 2017; he wrote nothing about AI. What is new
in 2026 is the speed of the "multitude". An agent writes a hypothesis, a script and a test in
minutes. His sentence was about one student in a laboratory; it now holds for every laboratory
that has an agent. This paragraph is my reading of him, not his.
