# The Chautauquas: "What's new?" and "What is best?"

What one source says, for the essay on the new and the better.

**What a Chautauqua was.** An adult education movement in the United States. It began in 1874
at Chautauqua Lake, in the state of New York, and spread through the rural country until the
mid-1920s, with "speakers, teachers, musicians, showmen, preachers, and specialists of the day"
(Wikipedia, "Chautauqua", revision 1368362770 of 8 August 2026).

**What Pirsig made of it.** His journey "is punctuated by numerous philosophical discussions,
referred to as Chautauquas by the author" (Wikipedia, "Zen and the Art of Motorcycle
Maintenance", revision 1380059406 of 13 October 2026). A Chautauqua of his is a talk that he
gives to the reader while he rides.

**The two questions.** In the first of them he says what the talks are for (chapter 1, as
Wikiquote gives it, revision 4029422 of 7 October 2026):

> "What's new?" is an interesting and broadening eternal question, but one which, if pursued
> exclusively, results only in an endless parade of trivia and fashion, the silt of tomorrow. I
> would like, instead, to be concerned with the question "What is best?," a question which cuts
> deeply rather than broadly, a question whose answers tend to move the silt downstream.

Three things to see in it:

1. He does not throw the first question away. It is "interesting and broadening". The fault is
   to follow it "exclusively".
2. The picture is a river. The new is silt that settles; the best is a current that moves it.
3. One question goes wide and the other goes deep.

**My word and his.** I say "the new versus the better". His word is "best". The essay keeps my
word in its title and his word in his quotation.
