# Why did Jung use dreams and myths to explain human psychology?

The owner's question. A first answer, from what Jung argued (as Wikipedia reports it):

- He saw the same mythic motifs in his patients' dreams and fantasies, also where the patient
  could hardly have known the myth. Snake dreams are "very common even among city-dwellers who
  have probably never seen a real snake" (*Collected Works* 8).
- So he proposed a **collective unconscious**, inherited and shared, with **archetypes** as its
  forms; myth is then "a sort of projection of the collective unconscious."
- His leading case: a patient's vision of a phallus hanging from the sun, which he matched to the
  *Mithras Liturgy*. Richard Noll (1997) later disputed the case.

The objection: Ray Scott Percival (1993) says the strong claims cannot be falsified, and a shared
world (day and night, families, animals) explains shared images without an inherited layer.
