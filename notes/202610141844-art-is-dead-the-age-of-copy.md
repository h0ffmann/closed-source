# Art is dead. The age of copy

**The thesis, mine.** Art is dead. This is the age of copy.

**What I set it against.** Gregory Wolfe's essays, *Beauty Will Save the World: Recovering the
Human in an Ideological Age* (ISI Books, 2011). The title comes from Dostoevsky's *The Idiot*,
by way of Solzhenitsyn's Nobel lecture, where Wolfe found the phrase. In the book's opening
chapter he calls beauty the "necessary agent for making the claims of truth and goodness
meaningful". I read that line in Jonathan Master's review of the book (The Englewood Review of
Books, 29 July 2011), not in the book itself, so the note is not verified at the source.

**Why the two belong together.** Wolfe's essays answer an ideological age with beauty. My
thesis says that the age we are in now answers everything with a copy. If beauty is what makes
truth and goodness meaningful, then an age that copies faster than it makes is not only an age
of cheap pictures: it is an age that can lose the agent Wolfe names. That is my reading, not his.

**Open question.** Is a copy that is credited, as the next note asks, still a copy in the sense
of this thesis, or is it the beginning of a new work? Wolfe's book does not answer it, and I have
not read whether he wrote about copying at all.
