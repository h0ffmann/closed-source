# Peer reviewing: the god that failed

My position, after a talk with prof2: peer review has failed. It failed because it started in
the wrong way, by the wrong person. Peer reviewing is a ritual.

**What the record shows, claim by claim.**

- **"It started in the wrong way."** Partly. Review by referees is old: "The first record of an
  editorial pre-publication peer-review is from 1665 by Henry Oldenburg", and the practice "did
  not become commonplace until the mid-20th-century"; "Nature itself instituted formal peer
  review only in 1967" (Wikipedia, "Scholarly peer review", revision
  [1378320468](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Scholarly_peer_review&oldid=1378320468)
  of 3 October 2026). So the review is three centuries old, and the rule that everybody must
  pass it is about as old as I am allowed to remember.
- **"By the wrong person."** What a person started is the business around it. Stephen Buranyi
  writes that "no one was more transformative and ingenious than Robert Maxwell, who turned
  scientific journals into a spectacular money-making machine"; that Maxwell called it "a
  perpetual financing machine"; that the checking, "a process known as peer review", "is done
  by working scientists on a volunteer basis"; and that after Maxwell's death in 1991 "an
  investigation revealed that he had stolen more than £400m from the Mirror pension fund"
  ("Is the staggeringly profitable business of scientific publishing bad for science?", *The
  Guardian*, 27 June 2017,
  <https://www.theguardian.com/science/2017/jun/27/profitable-business-scientific-publishing-bad-for-science>,
  read on 14 October 2026). That is one journalist's account, and it is about the journals,
  not about the first referee.
- **"Peer reviewing is a ritual."** Mine. It is a reading, and no source is asked for it. What
  would test it: a case where the rite was performed in full and the paper was false, and a
  case where a true paper never passed it.
- **Kubrick.** I call Stanley Kubrick "the peer reviewer destroyer", for his last film, which I
  hold to be his great work on secrets. The record: *Eyes Wide Shut* (1999), in which a doctor
  "infiltrates a masked orgy held by a secret society"; Kubrick "submitted his final cut of the
  film to Warner Bros. on March 1, 1999", and "died of a heart attack six days later"
  (Wikipedia, "Eyes Wide Shut", revision [1379745898](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Eyes_Wide_Shut&oldid=1379745898) of 11 October 2026). That the film is about peer review is my reading, not the film's own statement.
  He is thinker 39 of [`50THINKERS.md`](../50THINKERS.md).

**What this note does not say.** I asked for more links: from this business to Jeffrey Epstein,
and from him to intelligence services, to named politicians and businessmen, to paedophilia, to
Satanism. None of them is in this note. No source for them was fetched, and this repository
does not write an accusation against a named person without the source that records it and the
status of the case (AGENTS.md, invariants 2 and 5). A link of that kind enters one at a time:
the document, what exactly it shows, and whether a court decided it.

**Status: open question.** The history and the business are checked against one source each.
The verdict "failed" and the word "ritual" are my position.
