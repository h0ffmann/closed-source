# ASD-STE100 as a check: Vale

A paper's ASD-STE100 text is in `papers/<slug>/prose-ste/`. Before 12 November 2026, two things
kept it honest. The `/ste100` command told the writer the rules. `tools/fabric ste-check` failed
when the STE text lost a fact of `prose/`. No check read the rules themselves. Now Vale reads
them: the style `ste`, in `.vale/styles/ste/`.

```sh
just md-quality              # the .md check of CI: it includes Vale (ste) on papers/*/prose-ste/
just md-quality --self-test  # Vale (ste) must refuse a long sentence, a long paragraph, a phrasal verb
```

## The source of the rules

ASD-STE100 is a standard of ASD (the Aerospace, Security and Defence Industries Association of
Europe). Issue 9 is dated 15 January 2025 (<https://www.asd-ste100.org/>, read 12 November
2026). The specification is free, on request from ASD, and ASD keeps its copyright and its
trademark. So this repository copies no text and no dictionary entry of the specification. Each
rule below cites the public summary of Wikipedia, "Simplified Technical English"
(<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Technical_English>, read 12 November 2026), or this
repository's own `/ste100` command.

## The rules

| Rule | Source | Level | What it finds |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ste.SentenceLength` | "no more than 20 words in instructions (procedures) and 25 words in descriptive texts" | error | a sentence of more than 25 words; a paper is descriptive text |
| `ste.ParagraphLength` | "Do not write more than six sentences in each paragraph" | error | a paragraph of more than six sentences |
| `ste.PhrasalVerbs` | `/ste100`: "Do not use phrasal verbs" | error | `set up`, `find out`, `carry out` and others, each with one word to use |
| `ste.OneTerm` | `/ste100`: "Use one word for one meaning"; the terms of `STE100.md`, pass 1 | error | `proprietary` (the STE text says `closed source`), `PID` |
| `ste.IngForms` | "Use the -ing form of a verb only as a technical noun or as a modifier in a technical noun" | warning | a word in -ing that is not on the list of technical nouns |
| `ste.ComplexVerbs` | "Do not use auxiliary verbs to make complex verb constructions" | warning | a perfect (`has been`, `had found`) or a progressive (`is being`) |

An error has one fix, so it fails the `.md` check. A warning is advice: Vale cannot tell an -ing
verb from a technical noun, or a passive description from a perfect tense.

**Quotations are not counted.** `.vale.ini` hides each quotation, citation marker and heading
anchor from the rules (`TokenIgnores`). An STE pass never rewrites a quotation, and
`tools/fabric ste-check` fails if one changes. The self-test checks that a quotation of 30 words
passes.

**`fabric` too.** The STE text also gets the four rules of AGENTS.md (`md-quality.md`), because
it is English that this repository writes.

## The first run

On 12 November 2026, on `main` at `358636b`, Vale (ste) found 37 errors in 14 files of
`papers/closed-source/prose-ste/`: 23 paragraphs of more than six sentences and 14 sentences of
more than 25 words. All 37 are fixed in the same pull request. A paragraph was split at a change
of topic; a split **[ANALYSIS]** paragraph repeats its marker, which `ste-check` permits. A
sentence was split in two. No citation, quotation, number or anchor changed. The pass is in
`papers/closed-source/STE100.md`.

## The same rules in Agda

`agda/ClosedSource/STE/` states these rules as types and proves the properties that make the
check sound: for example, each long paragraph can be split with no sentence lost, and the
phrasal-verb table reaches a fixed point in one pass. `just agda` fails if the limits or the
table of this style and of the Agda differ ([agda.md](agda.md)).

## What Vale does not check

- the approved words of the STE dictionary, and their approved meanings: the dictionary is ASD's;
- if a sentence is a procedure (limit 20) or a description (limit 25);
- noun clusters of more than three words, and the active voice: a pattern cannot find them
  without many false alarms;
- if the STE text says what `prose/` says: `tools/fabric ste-check` checks the facts that it can
  count, and a person reads the rest.
